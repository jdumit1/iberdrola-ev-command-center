The urban charging network is growing fast — Spain has 38,000+ public chargepoints. But 98% of those are in cities. A driver going from Madrid to Galicia today relies on a patchwork of 847 interurban points, many of them slow AC chargers that do nothing for long-distance travel. By 2027, with 2.7 million EVs on the road, that gap becomes a crisis.

02 /Our Approach

Three data streams.
One integrated model.

What we combined

  • MITMA road network — 12 primary interurban corridors, ADT traffic data, POI anchors
  • datos.gob.es fork — EV fleet growth model, 2027 projection (mandatory)
  • NAP baseline — 847 existing interurban chargepoints to avoid redundancy
  • i-DE, Endesa, Viesgo — substation capacity datasets, MW available per node
  • DGT registrations — monthly EV registrations Jun–Nov 2025

How we optimized

  • Greedy coverage algorithm — advance along each corridor, place station at nearest viable anchor whenever the gap exceeds 140 km
  • Urban exclusion — 20-25 km radius around major city cores enforced
  • Junction merging — shared corridor nodes (Zaragoza, Sevilla) deduplicated
  • Grid classification — KD-tree spatial join to nearest substation within 40 km
  • Thresholds: ≥50 MW = Sufficient · 20-50 MW = Moderate · <20 MW = Congested

04 /Grid Bottlenecks

The grid is the real
constraint — not geography.

13 of our 52 proposed locations sit on substations with less than 20 MW available. These aren't marginal routes — they include Granollers (Barcelona's main freight interchange on A-7), Bailén (the gateway into Andalusia on A-4), and Alicante-Elche airport belt. You can pick the perfect location on a map, but if the substation can't support the load, the charger is decorative.

🔴 Most critical congested sites

  • Granollers (A-7) — 10 chargers, 1,500 kW demand · Endesa
  • Martorell (A-2) — 10 chargers, 1,500 kW demand · Endesa
  • Alicante-Elche (A-7) — 10 chargers, 1,500 kW demand · Endesa
  • Bailén (A-4) — 8 chargers, 1,200 kW demand · Endesa
  • Málaga W (A-7) — 8 chargers, 1,200 kW demand · Endesa
  • Algeciras (A-7) — 8 chargers, 1,200 kW · Endesa

💡 What to do about it

  • Iberdrola as DSO: On i-DE sites, Iberdrola can fast-track its own connection permits — regulatory advantage no competitor has
  • Endesa & Viesgo sites: Grid access agreements needed — begin negotiations now, 2025
  • Smart charging protocols: OCPP demand-response can extend Moderate sites to 8+ chargers within existing capacity
  • Total grid investment: ~€32.5M across 13 substations
05 /Deployment Strategy

Three phases.
One sequenced rollout.

Phase 1 — Deploy Now
2025–2026

  • 20 Sufficient-classified sites
  • 178 chargers, 26.6 MW
  • No grid works needed
  • All 10 corridors served
  • CAPEX ~€45M
  • AFIR compliance begins

Phase 2 — Managed Deploy
2026–2027

  • 19 Moderate sites
  • 143 chargers, 21.5 MW
  • OCPP smart charging
  • Staggered peak windows
  • Real-time load monitoring
  • CAPEX ~€36M

Phase 3 — Grid First
Parallel track 2025–2027

  • 13 Congested sites
  • ~€32.5M grid investment
  • i-DE self-funds own sites
  • Endesa/Viesgo agreements
  • Deployment follows works
  • AFIR deadline driver

The key insight: Phase 1 can start tomorrow. 20 sites, no waiting, every major corridor covered at minimum density. Phases 2 and 3 run simultaneously with different owners — Mobility deploys the chargers, i-DE runs the grid works. This isn't sequential; it's parallel.

06 /The Iberdrola Advantage

No one else can do
what Iberdrola can do here.

The dual-role advantage

Iberdrola is simultaneously the electricity distributor (i-DE) and the charging operator (Mobility) on 27 of the 52 proposed sites.

This means:

  • Self-approve connection requests — no waiting on a third-party DSO
  • Build grid works and chargers in parallel — 12-18 month faster deployment
  • Information advantage — Iberdrola knows its own substation headroom in real time
  • No margin leakage — grid costs stay within the group P&L

Business case at a glance

  • Total network CAPEX: ~€81M (chargers only)
  • Grid reinforcement: ~€32.5M (13 congested sites)
  • Revenue unlock at 2027 demand: 2.7M EVs × 1.3 kW AFIR ratio = 3.5 GW public power needed nationally
  • Iberdrola's interurban share: 61.8 MW of that demand at these 52 sites
  • AFIR compliance risk if NOT deployed: €50K per non-compliant km of TEN-T core network
  • First-mover advantage: Interurban charging brand loyalty is sticky — drivers return to familiar networks
