1. Executive Summary

We propose a network of 52 new charging stations and 400 chargers across Spain's primary interurban corridors, designed to eliminate range anxiety on every major route by 2027. The model integrates road traffic data (MITMA), the EV fleet growth projection from the mandatory datos.gob.es repository, and electrical substation capacity data from i-DE, Endesa, and Viesgo. The single most important finding is not where to put the chargers — it's that 13 of the 52 proposed locations require grid reinforcement before any charger can be switched on. Without that parallel infrastructure investment, the network cannot function.

2. Methodology

2.1 Scope Definition

The analysis is strictly limited to interurban roads as defined in the Ministry of Transport and Sustainable Mobility (MITMA) dataset — autopistas (AP-), autovías (A-), and carreteras nacionales (N-). All sites are verified to fall outside a 20–25 km exclusion radius of the eight largest Spanish city cores (Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Seville, Bilbao, Zaragoza, Málaga, Murcia), consistent with the datathon brief.

Grid threshold justification: A hub with 10 × 150 kW chargers draws 1.5 MW at peak. Engineering standards for new connection requests require substations to maintain a 10× safety factor across mixed-use loads during seasonal peaks. Spain's summer highway traffic runs 30–45% above annual average (MITMA 2024 Aforo data), meaning substations rated at their nominal capacity can face effective overload during August. The 20 MW / 50 MW thresholds were chosen conservatively to account for this seasonality.

2.3 Network Optimization Algorithm

Station placement follows a greedy coverage algorithm applied corridor-by-corridor:

  1. Extract POI anchors per corridor from MITMA road data: service areas, logistics nodes, regional city fringes, border crossings.
  2. Advance along the corridor from the Madrid origin. When the cumulative gap since the last placed station exceeds 140 km (10 km safety margin below the 150 km rule), place the next station at the nearest valid anchor.
  3. Merge overlapping coverage zones where corridors share a junction city (Zaragoza: A-2 / AP-68; Sevilla: A-4 / A-66) to eliminate redundant stations.
  4. Assign charger count: traffic tier × AFIR demand coefficient, rounded to nearest 2 chargers (minimum 6, maximum 12).

2.4 Grid Capacity Analysis

Each proposed site is spatially matched to its nearest electrical substation using a KD-tree join (ETRS89 / UTM Zone 30N projection, 40 km search radius). Distributor assignment follows geographic jurisdiction:

  • i-DE (Iberdrola): Central and northern interior — A-1, A-2 (western half), A-3, A-4 (north), A-5, A-6, A-66, AP-68
  • Endesa (e-distribución): Eastern and southern corridors — A-2 (Catalonia), A-7, A-4 (Andalusia), A-5 (Extremadura)
  • Viesgo: Cantabrian coast — A-8 corridor

4. Strategic Roadmap

Phase 1 — Deploy Now (2025–2026)
  • 20 Sufficient-classified sites
  • 178 chargers, 26.6 MW grid-ready
  • No reinforcement required
  • Covers all 10 corridors at minimum density
  • Estimated CAPEX: ~€45M
Phase 2 — Managed Deployment (2026–2027)
  • 19 Moderate sites with smart charging
  • 143 chargers, 21.5 MW with OCPP control
  • Staggered charging windows during peaks
  • Real-time substation load monitoring
  • Estimated CAPEX: ~€36M
Phase 3 — Grid-First (parallel track, 2025–2027)
  • 13 Congested sites requiring reinforcement
  • ~€32.5M grid investment (13 substations)
  • Charger deployment follows reinforcement
  • AFIR compliance depends on this track
  • Iberdrola as DSO can self-fund

The phasing logic is intentionally pragmatic. Phase 1 can start tomorrow — these 20 sites require no waiting on grid works, and they cover every major corridor at the minimum serviceable density. Phases 2 and 3 run simultaneously but with different owners: Iberdrola's charging business (Mobility) deploys Phase 2, while Iberdrola's distribution arm (i-DE) leads the grid reinforcement program. For Endesa and Viesgo sites in Phase 3, Iberdrola should enter bilateral grid access agreements as the charging operator.

4.1 The Killer Insight: Iberdrola's Dual Role

Something no competitor can replicate: Iberdrola is both the electricity distributor (i-DE) and the charging operator on 27 of the 52 proposed sites. This means Iberdrola can fast-track grid connection approvals, pre-negotiate substation access, and build the grid reinforcement and the charging station simultaneously rather than sequentially. The 13 Congested sites in i-DE territory (Calatayud, Utiel-Requena, Ponferrada, and others) are not a liability — they're sites where Iberdrola has an information advantage over every other charging operator.

5. Limitations and Data Gaps

  • Grid capacity data: The substation datasets from i-DE, Endesa, and Viesgo reflect published figures and may not capture recent connection requests that have reduced headroom. Grid classification should be re-validated against live data before final site selection.
  • EV projection: The 2,729,613 figure is a linear interpolation between 2024 actuals and the PNIEC 2030 target. Spain has historically underperformed EV targets, so this likely represents an optimistic scenario. A conservative 30% haircut would give ~1.9M EVs by 2027, which would reduce the charger deficit but not eliminate it.
  • Traffic seasonality: The MITMA ADT figures are annual averages. Summer peak demand (August) on the A-7 and A-4 corridors can be 40–60% higher, meaning Moderate stations may behave as Congested during peak weeks.
  • Existing station quality: The NAP baseline of 847 interurban chargers includes AC slow chargers (≤22 kW) alongside DC fast chargers. The effective interurban DC fast charger count is likely 150–200, making the gap significantly wider than the headline 847 suggests.
